# Foundations

AEE follows four operational stages:

1. **Frame** — identify the decision, claim boundaries, actors, and risk.
2. **Disassemble** — split conclusions into atomic claims and dependencies.
3. **Stress-test** — seek ambiguity, hidden assumptions, counterexamples, contradictions,
   missing evidence, circular inference, and unfalsifiable claims.
4. **Reconstruct** — narrow, clarify, gather evidence, resolve contradictions, or supersede.

## Claim model

A claim separates:

- its proposition from its source;
- its applicable boundary from universal language;
- observed evidence from inference and assertion;
- supporting evidence from counterevidence;
- direct confidence from dependency-propagated confidence; and
- current status from historical ledger events.

## Epistemic invariants

- A model's statement about its own work is `asserted`, not `observed`.
- A supported claim requires inspectable evidence.
- Contradictions are records to resolve, not noise to average away.
- An accepted claim needs a falsification condition.
- A dependent claim cannot be more certain than its weakest required premise.
- The absence of evidence is represented as insufficient evidence, not a fabricated answer.

## Deterministic and probabilistic work

The Python engine performs transparent deterministic checks. A model-backed reviewer may enrich
claims, propose alternatives, or locate counterevidence, but its output enters the same model and
remains labeled according to how it was obtained.
